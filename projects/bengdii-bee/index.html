Deliverables
Landing Page

Key Results

18% Landing-page conversion

Building the launch list

Bengdii was selling headphones to parents who had already bought headphones. The page had to establish a reason to switch.

Seven hours on a seven-⁠year-⁠old’s head

A new brand couldn’t win on audio quality against companies many times its size, so the page argued wear time: what seven hours of adult hardware does to a seven-year-old’s head.

Pre-orders, not Kickstarter

Children’s products have a poor record on Kickstarter, so we recommended pre-orders on Bengdii’s own site, timed to back to school — a deadline that couldn’t move.

Pre-Launch Landing Page

What I wrote and why.

Comfort as the Category

In a crowded market of brands touting sound quality, Bengdii’s lead benefit is comfort.

The Diagnosis

Names a problem the parent hadn’t: adult hardware on a child’s head all school day.

A Complaint With a Name

A vague ache becomes a condition a product can beat, and the loop beside it shows the gap the claim rests on.

Two Specs Written as Relief

The volume limiter and the contact-free earpieces are phrased as something taken away. The other four are plain positives.

The Headset Disappears

The first scenario argues absence — a child who stays social and present rather than sealed off — answering the isolation worry without restating it.

The Classroom, Moved Home

The second scenario is the school day itself, sold on the fatigue case above.

Safety, Shown on a Bike

Safety arrives as a scene: a father fitting the headset on a boy about to ride. The contact-free earpieces are the feature.

Ends on the Parent’s Goal

After a page built on relief-from, the closing line says what the relief is for.
