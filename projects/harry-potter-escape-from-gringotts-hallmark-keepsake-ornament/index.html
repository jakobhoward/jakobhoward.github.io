Our talented artists capture the essence of Harry Potter characters and scenes with a sophisticated eye for detail and unparalleled precision, immersing you in the film.

This spectacular set was brought to life by Keepsake Artists Jake Angell and Tim Bodendistel, working alongside Art Director Kurt Gaulke and a talented group of engineers, editors and sound & light designers.

A low rumble becomes a powerful growl as an imposing winged creature emerges from a massive cloud of dust. After years in captivity, the Ukrainian Ironbelly is finally liberated by Harry, Ron, and Hermione as they make their daring escape from Gringotts Wizarding Bank.

Details you’ll admire include the dragon’s deep red eyes, intricate scales, and its impressive 10-inch wingspan.

Quickly acting on Hermione’s clever plan, Harry Potter grips tightly to the dragon as the trio makes their escape. Fans will appreciate the incredible detail on his character, down to his iconic glasses.

Hermione’s iconic likeness has returned, with every detail artfully captured by our artists.

Ron demonstrates commendable bravery as he helps his friends make their epic escape, but you can still see the fear on his face as he grips the dragon for dear life.

What I wrote and why.

View the live campaign on Kickstarter ↗
135 characters to earn the scroll

Exclusivity, craftsmanship, and the idea that the ornament is a show rather than just a decoration all land before a single scroll.

Action!

The story opens by selling re-enactment, putting fans in the director’s chair for a scene they’ve rewatched for more than a decade.

‘Authentic’ and ‘Performance’

‘Authentic’ stakes a claim only an official license can back.

‘Performance’ turns the light and audio features into a show the ornament stages.

Craft Justifies Price

A licensed product is presumed mass-made; the copy proves otherwise. By the time the $199 price appears, the workmanship has already justified it.

People, not a logo

Naming the actual humans — two Keepsake Artists and an art director — turns ‘Hallmark’ from a corporate institution into an artisan studio.

The Scene, in Present Tense

Sound comes first: a rumble, a growl, then wings. A fan replays the scene as they read.

Detail at a glance

The densest information is also the fastest to read — no wall of text.

20 years of trust

Kickstarter backers read a first-time creator with no record as a risk signal. This line shuts that down.

Testimonials for past ornaments prove this is simply the next chapter — not a gamble.

Logistics as belonging

Urgency is the usual close, skipped here.The reason to order early is a manufacturing count: a stake in the batch.
