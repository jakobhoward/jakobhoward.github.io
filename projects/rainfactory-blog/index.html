Ecommerce Growth Page

What I wrote and why.

A department for hire

Many of the agency's clients had a founder doing the marketing between everything else. I pitched the agency as the department they hadn't hired yet.

The Differentiator, First

Data was Rainfactory's real edge over other agencies, so it gets the first section after the hero: the claim in one line, then the numbers as its proof.

Numbers first

The ROAS figure is a spread across clients rather than one best case.

The website line

I let the only mention of price on the page be a joke about it. The sentence after it is the retainer: pages keep getting tested and updated after the redesign ships.

The comparison

I wrote the right column as the habits a founder has already paid for once: opaque data, reporting by slide deck, a redesign followed by silence. Each cell on the left answers one.

rainfactory.com/services/launch-crowdfunding

Crowdfunding Services Page

What I wrote and why.

Proof in the subhead

How many launches the agency had already carried past $1M, and that no other agency matched it. The headline only has to name the job.

Tenure per platform

Three logos, three start years. Each date is a claim about tenure on that platform, and the third widens the offer past crowdfunding sites to a store the client owns.

Three steps

The intro leans on a phrase founders already use for pacing; the three chevrons then rename the stages in the agency’s words, and the last one is the tagline.

One Tile Swapped Per Audience

The same data module as the ecommerce page, with one tile changed: the ROAS spread becomes 50+ launches over $1 million.

Before the launch

The two steps before any campaign exists: a landing page on the client’s own domain, then paid traffic to it as the test bed for positioning and price. This was the work I did most often.

After the campaign

Step four is the retainer pitch, placed while the founder is still thinking about launch day. Its heading is the contract term itself, which answers the retainer objection up front.

A Client Who Is Also a Case Study

The testimonial is Onewheel's CEO, the account whose growth case study I wrote.

Why crowdfund at all

The only paragraph on the page that sells crowdfunding itself. The pitch is to a founder who needs investors: a campaign as public proof of demand.
