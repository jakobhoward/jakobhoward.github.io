My Role Senior Copywriter at
Client

Wrote two Omnilux landing pages: a B2B pitch to clinicians, and the launch page for Omnilux Men.

Deliverables
Provider Landing Page Omnilux Men Landing Page Facebook & Instagram Ads

Approach

Medical-grade light therapy

Omnilux builds award-winning LED light therapy devices that dermatologists and clinicians use for both medical and aesthetic work.

…Sold for the home

The objective: turn the practices that use these devices in office into resellers of the at-home line.

The readers knew more than the writer

The readers — dermatologists, aestheticians, health professionals — would know at once if the copy was guessing. So I learned the material first: clinical studies on LED light therapy, and a conversation with a contact in medical aesthetics.

A sales channel that didn’t exist before

The provider audience grew into 8% of Omnilux’s total email marketing list.

Provider Landing Page

What I wrote and why.

Business-to-business

The page sells clinics a new service to offer; the device is the means.

This section leans on institutional credibility: four university medical centers a clinician already trusts.

The business case

The headline sells revenue to the provider before results to their clients. Bullets underneath aim at the practice’s bottom line.

The Add-On Pitch

Microneedling, IPL, lasers, fillers, Botox, topicals: the section names the treatments a clinic already sells and positions the mask as an add-on to each.

Proof From a Peer

Clinics take a working esthetician’s word over a manufacturer’s, so the proof here is a peer on video.

The paragraph under it names the specific conditions she treats, so a provider can match it against their own client list.

Make It Feel Earned

The close is an application, not a checkout. Every applicant is reviewed before approval, so the provider ends up asking to be let in rather than being sold to.

Testimonial, then email signup

The last argument a wavering provider reads is another clinic recommending the device in its own words.

The final ask is an email and a phone number, so a provider who isn’t ready to apply still leaves a way to reach them.

Omnilux Men Landing Page

What I wrote and why.

Open on effort

I opened on the single-step simplicity because the audience was men who don’t keep a skincare routine.

Why “Engineered”

I chose “engineered” because it’s a masculine-coded word, whether it should be or not. It makes a skincare mask read as serious, practical technology.

The line the ads lifted

This paragraph names the complaints men already have, starting with the one their razor causes. Omnilux later ran it nearly word for word as ad copy.

A reason for a men’s version

“Thicker skin” gives a man a reason this mask is his, and the wavelength spec ties it to the hardware.

Close on the time cost

The closing section restates the hero’s promise as a time cost, the objection most likely to stop a first-time buyer.
