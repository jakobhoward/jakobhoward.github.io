‘Subscription’ was the wrong word

At this price ‘subscription’ carried every wrong association. Instead:

The default word

“Subscription”

One more recurring charge on a card
What shipped

“All-Access Household Membership”

One household, everything included, nothing metered

Let them put it in the room first

A 244-pound machine is a spatial decision as much as a financial one, so I pushed for a ‘see it in your space’ preview — and it shipped.

DTC Home Page

What I wrote and why.

Category Definer

How to introduce the first product in its category? When I described it to friends as “Peloton for Pilates,” it clicked instantly. The hero had to land the same way, but without borrowed names.

Answering Price First

Financing does the heavy lifting for a $5,000 machine. The monthly figure sits in gym-membership territory, so price is settled before the specs arrive.

Twelve Parts, Named

Grip mats, touch screen, resistance labeled on the machine. At this price, the parts list is the proof of build.

Three Ways In

Naming three distinct fitness component goals lets every reader find their own reason to buy.

The Other Half of the Price

Named trainers and a filterable class library prove the other half of the price — the coaching — actually exists.

Spec → Outcome

I turned sensor hardware into something a buyer can feel: live feedback mid-workout, on devices they already own.

What the Membership Buys

Listing live classes, programs, and real-time My Score coaching ties actual value to the monthly fee to keep it from feeling like rent.

Ending on Stronger

The close returns to emotion: “You’re one workout away from Stronger.”

reformrx.com/products/reformrx-reformer

DTC Product Page

What I wrote and why.

Answer Price From Every Angle

A struck-through retail price, $500 savings, and a Klarna monthly together make ordering now the prudent move.

The renamed term earns its spot here: a buyer reads who’s covered before they read what it costs.

Boxing white-glove delivery under its own icon was my call: installation reads as part of the offer.

The Living-Room Question

The AR module answers the size question in the buyer’s own space.

The capture itself is mine, shot on my iPhone in our Seattle office.

Name the Real Competitor

The ladder prices the studio habit — classes, private sessions, coaching — and the do-it-yourself reformer, so the buyer’s own routine becomes the competitor.

After three X’d rows, the machine gets the ladder’s only checkmark: one figure, paid once.
